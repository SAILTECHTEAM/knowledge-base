# I3D (Inflated 3D ConvNet)

**Paper:** Joao Carreira and Andrew Zisserman, Quo Vadis, Action Recognition? A New Model and the Kinetics Dataset, CVPR 2017.

**Paper link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07750

**Code:** https://github.com/deepmind/kinetics-i3d

![Title header of the I3D paper with authors from DeepMind and Oxford](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAILTECHTEAM/knowledge-base/main/static/img/cv_img/I3Dimg/image1.png)

## Background: Two Contributions

### A New Model, I3D, Was Proposed

The central question is how to expand a mature 2D image model into a 3D video model. A good expansion reuses the architecture and the pretrained weights of the 2D network, so training stays simple and ImageNet pretraining is not wasted.

### The Kinetics Dataset Was Designed

Video research lacked a large-scale dataset comparable to ImageNet. The authors released Kinetics-400: 400 human action classes with more than 400 clips per class, around 300,000 clips in total, each about 10 seconds long and drawn from YouTube. The authors also argue that video models should be pretrained on video rather than images alone, and Kinetics makes that pretraining possible.

![A single film still whose action is ambiguous without surrounding frames](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAILTECHTEAM/knowledge-base/main/static/img/cv_img/I3Dimg/image2.png)

A single image often cannot settle the action label. The surrounding frames supply the motion that disambiguates it, which is why the paper treats video modeling as a joint spatiotemporal problem.

## Preliminary: Optical Flow

Optical flow estimates per-pixel displacement between consecutive frames and serves as an explicit motion signal. It is useful when the task depends on how objects move over time rather than on static appearance.

The standard formulation rests on three assumptions:

- Brightness constancy: a tracked point keeps its intensity between frames.
- Small motion: displacement between two frames is modest.
- Spatial coherence: neighboring pixels move in a similar way.

Two limitations follow directly. Flow fields do not always match true object motion, because the computation tracks apparent brightness patterns. The method is also sensitive to lighting: when illumination changes, brightness constancy breaks and recognition degrades. Dense flow is additionally expensive, since a displacement vector is estimated for nearly every pixel.

![Optical flow vectors and camera-motion-compensated flow vectors on a running person](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAILTECHTEAM/knowledge-base/main/static/img/cv_img/I3Dimg/image5.jpeg)

## I3D

The paper compares five architectures on the same footing before arriving at I3D.

![Five video architectures compared in the paper: LSTM, 3D-ConvNet, two-stream, 3D-fused, and two-stream I3D](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SAILTECHTEAM/knowledge-base/main/static/img/cv_img/I3Dimg/image6.png)

### Architecture a) CNN Plus LSTM

Each frame passes through a 2D CNN, and the resulting sequence enters an LSTM for temporal modeling. Feature extraction and temporal reasoning stay separate: the CNN never sees motion, and the LSTM receives only frame-level summaries. Space and time therefore do not interact during feature learning, and the accuracy on video benchmarks stays modest.

### Architecture b) 3D ConvNet

Consecutive frames form a 3D volume over time, height, and width. 3D convolutions learn space and time jointly, with pooling to control the size of the 3D feature maps and fully connected layers for classification. The drawback is pretraining: ordinary 2D networks inherit ImageNet weights directly, while this 3D network starts from scratch, which makes training harder and overfitting more likely.

### Architecture c) Two-Stream Network

Two 2D networks run in parallel, one on RGB frames and one on precomputed optical flow. ImageNet pretraining applies to both branches, and motion modeling comes from the flow input rather than 3D convolution. Flow here is not a true temporal axis; it is precomputed displacement fed to a 2D network. Fusion happens only at the score level, so the two streams never share features.

### Architectures d) and e)

Both combine RGB and flow, and they differ in where and how fusion happens.

Model (d) keeps the two 2D streams but fuses high-level feature maps into a spatiotemporal volume that enters a small 3D network. Fusion moves before the final decision, which improves accuracy, but the pipeline stays heavy: dense flow must still be precomputed, and a 3D stage is added on top.

Model (e) is I3D. RGB and flow each enter their own 3D network, every branch models space and time jointly, and the two streams fuse at the end:

> Each branch performs spatiotemporal modeling independently, followed by late fusion of the two results.

The table below, taken from the paper, shows the cost of each design. I3D carries 25M parameters against 79M for the plain 3D-ConvNet, while its test-time temporal footprint reaches the full 10 seconds.

### ImageNet Pretraining and Transfer

Pretraining on a large dataset such as ImageNet lets a network learn transferable features for smaller tasks. Before Kinetics, the video field had no equivalent: video models were usually trained from scratch on small datasets such as UCF-101 or HMDB-51. I3D plus Kinetics closes this gap by giving video the same pretrain-then-fine-tune workflow that images already had.

## Method

### How to Inflate

Inflation keeps the overall architecture fixed and extends each 2D operation into 3D:

- 2D convolutions become 3D convolutions, and 2D pooling becomes 3D pooling. A $k \times k$ filter becomes $k \times k \times k$ over time, height, and width.
- Temporal stride and pooling are set so the receptive field grows gradually along time, matching how the spatial receptive field grows with depth.

### Bootstrapping 3D Filters From 2D

The goal is to initialize the 3D network from a pretrained 2D network so that both produce the same response on a still image repeated into a static video.

The check works as follows. Repeat one image along time to form a boring video in which every frame is identical. Run the original 2D network on the image and the inflated 3D network on the boring video. A correct initialization gives the same output for both.

The construction repeats each 2D filter $N$ times along the temporal axis, where $N$ is the temporal size of the inflated filter, and divides the weights by $N$. The division matters: without it, the $N$ copies would sum $N$ identical responses and overshoot the 2D output by a factor of $N$. With it, the 3D network starts exactly where the 2D network left off and then learns temporal structure during training on Kinetics.

## Results in Brief

Pretrained on Kinetics and fine-tuned on small benchmarks, the two-stream I3D reaches about 98.0 percent on UCF-101 and about 80.9 percent on HMDB-51 as reported in the paper, substantially above the same architectures trained from scratch. The gain confirms the paper's claim: video models, like image models, benefit most from large-scale pretraining in their own modality.

:::note[Scope of this note]
EfficientNet-style compound scaling of depth, width, and resolution is intentionally left out here. It postdates I3D by two years and plays no role in the inflation argument.
:::
