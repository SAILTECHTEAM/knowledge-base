# RT-DETR and RT-DETRv2: Latency Budgets and Operator Constraints

Across the preceding nine installments, the technical evolution of the DETR family revolved around a single constraint chain of **accuracy and training convergence**: resolving the compute overhead of global dense attention and multi-scale processing ([Deformable DETR](./deformable-detr)); explicitly establishing four-dimensional geometric anchor boxes to eliminate localization cold start ([Conditional and DAB-DETR](./conditional-dab-detr)); suppressing early matching oscillation through a denoising bypass ([DN-DETR](./dn-detr), [DINO](./dino)); and expanding positive-sample supervision density during training through query grouping ([Group DETR](./group-detr)) and target synthesis ([DEIM](./deim)).

However, all of these improvements share one implicit subtext in their optimization objective: **trading fewer or fixed training epochs for higher detection accuracy (AP)**. When models are pushed toward industrial deployment, the evaluation criterion shifts fundamentally — the system's core objective function changes from "how many epochs to converge" to **"how high an AP can the model output within the millisecond-level latency budget given by the hardware"**.

In the real-time object detection domain, convolutional detectors represented by the YOLO family have long held a dominant position. Although the DETR architecture carries a structural advantage in theory — "end-to-end set prediction, naturally free of non-maximum suppression (NMS) post-processing" — it has long lagged severely in end-to-end inference speed.

This article focuses on **RT-DETR (Zhao et al., CVPR 2024)** and its follow-up technical update **RT-DETRv2 (Lv et al., 2024)**. Going down into the forward computation graph and operator execution layer of physical hardware, it dissects the three core physical latency bottlenecks of traditional DETR architectures and analyzes how the author teams rebuilt an industrial-grade real-time end-to-end detection system by reshaping encoder compute allocation, aligning initial query quality, reconstructing the sampling operator, and streamlining the training recipe.

## Motivation: Benchmark Alignment and the Triple Latency Bottleneck of Non-Real-Time DETR

### Establishing the Evaluation Protocol: End-to-End Discipline Including NMS under TensorRT FP16

Discussing real-time detection requires first unifying the units of measurement. Prior academic comparisons suffered from widespread protocol misalignment:

1. **Misaligned input resolution:** traditional DETR models habitually use large $(800, 1333)$ inputs to pursue high AP, while real-time detectors universally standardize on $640 \times 640$;
2. **Post-processing time deliberately excluded:** many convolutional detectors report FPS by counting only the forward time of the backbone and prediction head, excluding NMS post-processing time on CPU or CUDA.

The RT-DETR authors established a strict testing discipline for their benchmark: **on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU, using a TensorRT FP16 engine, with input size fixed at $640 \times 640$, batch size 1, and measured latency including the full end-to-end pipeline.**

Once this standard was set, the authors used measurements to expose a long-standing misconception: **the claim "DETR has no NMS, so inference is faster" does not naturally hold in engineering practice**. At $640 \times 640$ input, a tuned efficient NMS operator (such as TensorRT's `EfficientNMS`) typically takes only $1.0 \sim 2.5\text{ ms}$ in common scenarios. By comparison, the model's own forward latency (DINO-Deformable-DETR-R50 takes tens or even hundreds of milliseconds, reaching only about 5 FPS) is the absolute main obstacle to real-time operation.

To bring DETR genuinely into the real-time range, the specific bottlenecks in the model's forward computation graph that consume compute time and hardware bandwidth must be disassembled.

### Bottleneck Anatomy: Encoder Sequence Explosion, Decoder Depth Lock-In, and the Memory-Access Dead End of Interpolation Operators

Combined with hardware profiler analysis, the inference latency bottlenecks of non-real-time DETR models sit precisely at three physical stages:

| Bottleneck location | Physical phenomenon and performance loss | Structural cause | RT-DETR / v2 improvement entry point |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Encoder: redundant high-resolution feature processing** | The encoder consumes **49% of the model's GFLOPs** yet contributes only 11% of AP; multi-scale sequences reach thousands of tokens, and stacked self-attention layers cause severe compute and memory-access congestion. | Intra-scale interaction and cross-scale feature fusion are computed together in one pass; shallow, low-semantic tokens blindly participate in dense self-attention. | **Efficient hybrid encoder (AIFI + CCFM)**: self-attention is kept only on the highest-semantic layer $S_5$; shallow interaction is handed entirely to convolutions. |
| **2. Decoder: the latency floor of six serial refinement layers** | The decoder executes layer by layer serially, each layer costing a stable ~$0.5\text{ ms}$; the layer count cannot be compressed — cutting decoder layers directly collapses AP. | Query initialization depends only on classification scores, selecting many low-quality candidates that "classify high but localize poorly", forcing the decoder to correct them step by step through stacked layers. | **IoU-aware Query Selection**: IoU soft labels align classification with geometry in the optimization objective, shifting the localization burden forward and turning decoder depth into a **training-free speed knob**. |
| **3. Low-level operators: irregular memory access and deployment dead ends** | Deformable Attention's paper FLOPs are low, but measured execution is slow; the underlying `grid_sample` operator is severely blocked on dedicated inference engines and embedded NPUs. | Bilinear interpolation driven by floating-point offsets is irregular, jumping memory access; it lacks general operator-fusion support and quantizes poorly to fixed point. | **Discrete sampling operator (`discrete_sample`)**: rounds floating-point offsets into integer indices, eliminating interpolation overhead and removing the hardware deployment barrier. |

## Architecture Mechanism: The Efficient Hybrid Encoder Decouples Intra-Scale and Cross-Scale Interaction

### Structural Evolution Ablation: From Full-Scale Multi-Scale Attention to Intra-Scale Local Interaction

In Deformable DETR and its successors (such as DINO), to detect objects at different scales, the backbone's $\{S_3, S_4, S_5\}$ feature maps are flattened and concatenated into one long sequence fed to the multi-scale Transformer encoder. At $640 \times 640$ input, the spatial sizes per scale are:

- $S_3$ (stride 8): $80 \times 80 = 6400$ tokens
- $S_4$ (stride 16): $40 \times 40 = 1600$ tokens
- $S_5$ (stride 32): $20 \times 20 = 400$ tokens

Concatenated, the sequence length reaches $8400$. Even though a single deformable-attention layer reduces theoretical complexity to a linear function of the number of sampling points, the memory access cost (MAC) involved in self-attention mapping over high-resolution features, sampling-point coordinate prediction, and feature aggregation remains enormous.

The RT-DETR paper designed a set of strictly progressive encoder architecture variants (Variants A through E) to systematically strip redundant computation from the encoder:

![Evolution of encoder variants: A removes the encoder entirely; B runs shared-weight single-scale self-attention independently per scale; C adds cross-scale concatenation; D explicitly decouples intra-scale interaction from CNN-based cross-scale fusion; E converges into the hybrid encoder](/img/cv_img/detr-series/rtdetr_variants.png)

Combined with the paper's ablation data on a T4 GPU, this progression reveals a key physical pattern:

| Variant | Encoder design | AP (%) | Latency (ms) | Conclusion and mechanism attribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Variant A** | Remove the Transformer encoder entirely | 43.0 | 7.2 | Losing encoder feature enhancement yields a low baseline. |
| **Variant B** | Shared single-layer self-attention, intra-scale interaction per scale | 44.9 | 11.1 | Intra-scale interaction adds +1.9 AP, but sequence processing inflates latency by 54%. |
| **Variant C** | Concatenate all scales and perform full-scale interaction | 45.6 | 13.3 | Cross-scale fusion adds a further +0.7 AP, but full multi-scale attention raises latency to 13.3 ms. |
| **Variant D** | **Explicit decoupling**: intra-scale self-attention first, then PANet-style convolutional cross-scale fusion | 46.4 | 12.2 | **Decoupling wins twice**: AP up 0.8% while latency drops 8%. |
| **Variant $D_{S_5}$** | **Keep intra-scale self-attention only on the top layer $S_5$**; other scales go straight into convolutional fusion | 46.8 | **7.9** | **Breakthrough inflection**: latency plunges 35% (12.2 ms to 7.9 ms) while AP actually rises 0.4%. |
| **Variant E (RT-DETR)** | Carefully designed RepBlock fusion blocks on top of $D_{S_5}$ (the full hybrid encoder) | **47.9** | 9.3 | Local re-parameterized convolutions push representation capacity at a reasonable latency cost. |

### Division of Labor: Entity-Level Modeling on S5 and Local Convolutional Aggregation on Shallow Layers

The Variant $D_{S_5}$ results form the core theoretical basis of RT-DETR's encoder design: **why does removing all self-attention on shallow features ($S_3, S_4$) slash latency yet raise accuracy rather than lower it?**

The answer lies in the inherent hierarchical property of deep convolutional features:

1. **Differences in semantic abstraction:** shallow feature maps (such as $S_3$) have high resolution, and their tokens mainly carry local low-level geometric textures and edges. These tokens lack clear high-level semantic concepts. Running cross-spatial global attention among thousands of shallow tokens not only incurs quadratic compute cost but also easily introduces large amounts of spurious high-frequency attention associations between local textures and irrelevant background;
2. **High-level features carry concept entities:** in contrast, the top layer $S_5$, after repeated downsampling, has a spatial resolution of only $20 \times 20 = 400$. Each token's receptive field already covers most of the image and represents highly abstract entity-level semantic concepts. Running self-attention among these 400 tokens efficiently models global co-occurrence relations and contextual constraints between objects, at very low compute cost;
3. **Cross-scale transfer is more efficient with local convolution:** cross-scale semantic supplementation (high-level semantics guiding shallow localization; shallow detail feeding back to high levels) is essentially spatially local feature reuse. Using heavily hardware-optimized convolutions with contiguous memory access and local inductive bias (such as PANet-style top-down and bottom-up fusion paths) for cross-scale fusion vastly outperforms attention operators in hardware execution efficiency.

On this basis, RT-DETR established the two-module **efficient hybrid encoder**:

- **AIFI (Attention-based Intra-scale Feature Interaction):** applies a single standard multi-head self-attention (MHSA) layer only to the $S_5$ features, with identical input and output dimensions;
- **CCFM (CNN-based Cross-scale Feature-fusion Module):** built from convolutions, combining re-parameterized convolution blocks (RepBlock) and skip connections to perform multi-scale bidirectional fusion across $\{S_3, S_4, F_5\}$ (see Appendix A for the detailed tensor-flow derivation).

## Mechanism Reconstruction: IoU-Aware Query Selection Resolves the Classification–Localization Disconnect

### Defect Localization: Distribution Mismatch When Selecting Candidates by Classification Confidence Alone

In [DINO](./dino), mixed query selection established the effective paradigm of "selecting the Top-$K$ proposals from the encoder's top layer to initialize the decoder's 4D anchor boxes". However, in DINO and every DETR model before it, the criterion for selecting these Top-$K$ features depended **entirely on the classification head's predicted confidence (Classification Score)**.

Analyzing the detection head's outputs, the RT-DETR paper points out: **there is a severe distribution misalignment (Inconsistent Distribution) between predicted classification scores and the true localization quality (IoU) of the boxes**.

![Scatter plot of classification scores versus IoU: the x-axis is the ground-truth IoU score and the y-axis the predicted classification confidence. Pink points are outputs under traditional query selection; dark blue points are outputs with the IoU-aware constraint](/img/cv_img/detr-series/rtdetr_iou_aware.png)

The scatter plot clearly shows two phenomena:

1. **Inflated classification with poor geometry (dense pink points in the lower right):** some feature tokens score as high as 0.8 in classification while their predicted boxes have IoU below 0.1 against the ground truth. The classification head responds to "whether salient foreground local texture exists", but the regressed box for that texture may completely miss the object boundary;
2. **Accurate localization with low classification:** many features geometrically well aligned with the ground truth (IoU $>0.8$) sit in non-central regions of the object, score lower in classification, and are directly discarded in the Top-$K$ ranking.

This disconnect floods the first decoder layer's initial anchor boxes with candidates carrying large spatial errors. The decoder is forced to rely on deeply stacked cross-attention layers, correcting these wayward anchors one layer at a time.

### Mechanism Reconstruction: Injecting True IoU as the Target Value into the Classification Branch's Soft Loss

To make the encoder's Top-$K$ proposals account for both classification confidence and geometric precision, RT-DETR proposes **IoU-aware Query Selection**.

The mechanism's core is modifying the training-time optimization objective of the classification branch on encoder features, explicitly injecting the intersection-over-union (IoU) between predicted and ground-truth boxes as a continuous soft label into the supervised loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{encoder}}(\hat{y}, y) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{box}}(\hat{b}, b) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}(\hat{c}, c, \text{IoU})
$$

Previously, the classification supervision target for positive samples was a deterministic one-hot scalar ($c \in \{0, 1\}$). Under IoU-aware supervision, when the overlap between the predicted box $\hat{b}$ and the ground-truth box $b$ is low, the classification loss incurs a penalty even if the feature correctly hits the object class, because the target is scaled to the actual $\text{IoU}$ value; only when **the class is predicted correctly and the box fits the ground-truth object tightly** can the classification branch earn a full reward (see Appendix B for the exact loss formulation).

### Verification: Doubling the Share of High-Confidence High-IoU Queries and Zero-Overhead Accuracy Gains

This consistency constraint forces the encoder to autonomously build a combined credibility measure in feature space. In the scatter plot, the dark blue points (with the IoU-aware constraint) show a clear contraction toward the upper-right corner:

- **Share of high-classification, high-IoU (both metrics $>0.5$) quality queries:** surges from **0.30% under the traditional mechanism to 0.67%** (a relative increase of 120%);
- **Final whole-model accuracy gain:** a direct **+0.8% AP** algorithmic gain on COCO val2017, while this mechanism introduces no extra floating-point computation or network structure at inference time.

## Deployment Mechanism: Shifting the Localization Burden Forward and a Retraining-Free Flexible Decoder

### Diminishing Returns: Deep-Layer Refinement Saturates as Initial Query Quality Improves

In traditional detectors, adjusting inference speed usually requires redesigning backbones of different depths and widths (for example, moving from YOLOv8-N to YOLOv8-X), with each switch requiring a full training run. In the DETR family, by contrast, every decoder layer carries an independent auxiliary prediction head during training and shares a unified output space.

Thanks to the high-quality hybrid encoder features and IoU-aware Query Selection, **the decoder already obtains a highly confident spatial addressing starting point from layer 1 onward**. The "coarse localization search" burden that traditional DETRs must place on the first 3 decoder layers is substantively transferred into the single-layer AIFI encoder and the upstream filtering mechanism.

This transfer directly reshapes the per-layer decoder benefit curve. The RT-DETR paper ablates the 6-layer decoder layer by layer at inference:

| Decoder layers used at inference | AP (%) | Loss vs. full depth | Total inference latency (ms) | Time saved (ms) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Layer 6 (full baseline)** | **53.1** | - | 9.3 | - |
| **Layer 5** | 53.0 | $\downarrow 0.1$ | 8.8 | $\mathbf{0.5}$ |
| **Layer 4** | 52.7 | $\downarrow 0.4$ | 8.3 | $\mathbf{1.0}$ |
| **Layer 3** | 52.4 | $\downarrow 0.7$ | 7.9 | $\mathbf{1.4}$ |
| **Layer 2** | 51.3 | $\downarrow 1.8$ | 7.5 | $\mathbf{1.8}$ |
| **Layer 1** | 49.1 | $\downarrow 4.0$ | 7.0 | $\mathbf{2.3}$ |

### Production Deployment: Runtime On-Demand Decoder Truncation as a Dynamic Gear Shift

Two key engineering conclusions can be drawn from the ablation data:

1. **Linear determinism of per-layer decoder cost:** on T4 hardware, each serial Transformer decoder layer adds an extremely stable ~**0.5 ms**;
2. **Sharply diminishing marginal returns in deeper layers:** as depth increases, the refinement gain between adjacent layers converges rapidly. Rolling back from layer 6 to layer 5 causes only a 0.1% AP statistical fluctuation yet steadily releases 0.5 ms of compute time.

This means that in real engineering deployments, **no retraining or weight-export restructuring is required**. Trained 6-layer weights deploy as-is; when facing high-throughput burst traffic or lightweight edge hardware, the inference runtime simply reads the tensor output of the layer-4 or layer-5 auxiliary prediction head and skips the forward computation of subsequent attention layers, achieving a continuous, smooth dynamic "accuracy–latency" gear shift.

## Operator Reconstruction: Discrete Sampling Removes the Memory-Access and Deployment Dead Ends of Continuous Grid Interpolation

If RT-DETR v1 conquered FLOPs allocation at the model architecture level, then one year later **RT-DETRv2** turned the blade directly toward the submerged rocks of engineering deployment — **hardware operator compatibility and physical memory-access bottlenecks**.

### Operator Bottleneck: Irregular Memory Access from Continuous Bilinear Interpolation Blocks Hardware Deployment

In academic research, deformable attention is usually treated as an efficient sparse operator. The network predicts floating-point relative offsets $(\Delta x, \Delta y)$ through linear layers, then uses bilinear interpolation (implemented in PyTorch via `torch.nn.functional.grid_sample` or custom CUDA operators) to sample continuously from the feature map.

On the industrial deployment side, however (especially exporting ONNX to TensorRT or OpenVINO, or deploying to embedded NPU chips such as Rockchip RKNN, Horizon BPU, or Ascend CANN), the `grid_sample` operator causes a thorny engineering disaster:

1. **Discontiguous memory access (Memory-Bound):** floating-point offsets scatter sampling points in irregular jumps across physical memory addresses, yielding very low GPU L1/L2 cache hit rates. Although its arithmetic load (FLOPs) is far smaller than regular convolution, actual execution time on the chip remains stubbornly high;
2. **Fragmented hardware operator-library support:** most edge NPU chips provide highly hardened acceleration for convolution operators but lack native hardware units for dynamic grid sampling with bilinear interpolation, forcing fallback to general-purpose CPU software execution and collapsing pipeline latency instantly;
3. **Difficult fixed-point quantization:** continuous interpolation is prone to numerical overflow and distribution drift under INT8 symmetric/asymmetric quantization, causing cliff-like accuracy drops after quantization.

### Discrete Integer Sampling: Coordinate Rounding and Truncating Offset Gradients during Fine-Tuning

Addressing this pain point, RT-DETRv2 made an extremely decisive engineering simplification: **abandon bilinear interpolation entirely and introduce a discrete sampling operator (Discrete Sampling)**.

Its computation logic is blunt: round the continuous relative offsets predicted by the head into integer pixel indices:

$$
(\Delta x_{\text{int}}, \Delta y_{\text{int}}) = \text{Round}(\Delta x, \Delta y)
$$

Sampling then reads values directly from the feature map's integer-coordinate memory cells, completely skipping the weighted computation of bilinear interpolation.

:::info[Question]

**Rounding is not differentiable — how does backpropagation update the offset prediction head?**

The rounding function's derivative is zero everywhere in the real domain, so end-to-end training would leave the linear layer predicting the offsets completely without gradients.

RT-DETRv2 adopts a pragmatic **two-stage training scheme**:

1. **Full pre-training stage:** for the first 72 epochs, the model keeps the original `grid_sample` for standard training, letting the network fully learn the optimal sampling geometry around objects in continuous space;
2. **Fine-tuning truncation stage:** during the final 12 epochs (the 1x configuration) of fine-tuning, the operator is replaced with `discrete_sample` while **gradient backpropagation to the offset-prediction linear layer is cut off (turn off gradient)**; only the classification head, content attention, and other backbone parameters adaptively fine-tune against the discrete features.

:::

Testing shows that replacing continuous with discrete sampling costs only an extremely slight accuracy retreat on the most demanding COCO $\text{AP}_{\text{val}}$ (models of all sizes generally drop only $0.1 \sim 0.4\%$) with almost no loss on $\text{AP}_{50}$, while completely eliminating the deployment barrier of depending on a specialized operator.

### Redistributing the Sampling Budget: Non-Uniform Sampling across Resolutions and a 75% Cut in Sampling Points

Another implicit setting inherited from the original Deformable DETR is: **allocating exactly the same number of sampling points on every feature scale (4 points per head per scale by default)**.

RT-DETRv2 points out that high-resolution and low-resolution features differ enormously in information redundancy, and forcing uniform sampling creates unnecessary memory-bandwidth overhead. By setting differentiated sampling-point counts per scale in the decoder, the authors compressed the total sampling points of a single forward pass from the baseline's 86,400 to 21,600 (a 75% reduction); AP on COCO dropped only a marginal 0.6% while memory-access bandwidth was substantially released at the hardware level.

## Evidence and Attribution: End-to-End Speed–Accuracy Comparison and the Truth about Small-Model Underfitting

Beyond structural and operator-level fixes, RT-DETRv2 also made two key adjustments to the training recipe and gained significantly on small models:

### Dynamic Data Augmentation: Disabling Strong Geometric and Photometric Distortion at the End of Training

In the engineering practice of single-stage detectors (such as YOLOv5 and YOLOv8), complex Mosaic and Mixup augmentations are usually turned off near the end of training so the weights adapt to undistorted object appearances under the true distribution.

RT-DETRv2 systematically introduces this strategy into the DETR family:

- **Strong augmentation early:** the first phase of training keeps high-intensity geometric perturbations including color distortion (RandomPhotometricDistort), large-range zoom-out (RandomZoomOut), and random cropping (RandomIoUCrop), improving representational generalization;
- **Dynamic disabling in the last two epochs:** in the final 2 epochs, all four strong perturbations are turned off and fine-tuning uses undistorted original-scale inputs, helping the prediction head and initial queries quickly fit the true aspect-ratio and texture priors of the target domain.

### Tiered Backbone Learning Rates: Optimizer Step Sizes Adapted to Model Capacity

In the original RT-DETR, all model sizes (from the lightweight ResNet18 to the large ResNet101) used identical learning-rate hyperparameters. v2 realigns backbone learning rates by model scale:

- Lightweight backbones (such as ResNet18): feature extraction capacity is naturally weak, and transferring pretrained features to detection requires a more aggressive update step; the authors raise the backbone learning rate to `1e-4`;
- Heavy backbones (such as ResNet101): already possessing high data-fitting and representation capacity, large steps easily damage pretrained weights, so the backbone learning rate is strictly suppressed to `1e-6`.

### Attribution: Repairing Underfitting in Lightweight Small Models while Gains Converge on Large Models

Observing the accuracy transitions of RT-DETR and RT-DETRv2 across model scales (table below), the benefit boundary of this training recipe becomes clear:

| Model size | Backbone | RT-DETR AP (%) | RT-DETRv2 AP (%) | Absolute net gain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **S** | ResNet18 | 46.5 | **47.9** | **$\mathbf{+1.4}$** |
| **M** | ResNet34 | 48.9 | **49.9** | **$\mathbf{+1.0}$** |
| **M\*** | ResNet50 | 51.3 | **51.9** | $\mathbf{+0.6}$ |
| **L** | ResNet50 | 53.1 | **53.4** | $\mathbf{+0.3}$ |
| **X** | ResNet101 | 54.3 | **54.3** | **$\mathbf{+0.0}$** |

From S to X, the gain shows a monotonically narrowing trend from $+1.4\%$ down to $+0.0\%$.

This data reveals a fact usually obscured by engineering reports: **the introduction of dynamic data augmentation and tiered learning rates is not a universal breakthrough beyond the network's capacity ceiling; in essence it repairs the underfitting defect of lightweight small models under short-cycle training**. For the parameter-rich X-size model, network capacity is already sufficient to absorb the complex perturbation distribution, and recipe adjustments cannot push the accuracy ceiling any higher.

## Limitations and Implications: The Cost Boundary of Real-Time Trade-offs and Unclosed Evidence

RT-DETR and RT-DETRv2 set a new benchmark in real-time detection, but as technical analysis the compromises behind their real-time performance and the unresolved technical boundaries must be viewed objectively:

### Context Loss for Dense Small Objects from Stripping Shallow Attention

Completely removing self-attention on shallow features ($S_3, S_4$) eliminates large computational redundancy, but geometrically it also abandons whole-image long-range relational modeling on high-resolution features. For extremely dense clusters of tiny objects (such as dense crowds from a micro-UAV perspective) or objects with extreme aspect ratios, CCFM's reliance entirely on local convolution still faces a physical limit in contextual awareness range.

### Evidence Gap in Physical Acceleration Data for the Discrete Sampling Operator

Although RT-DETRv2 proposed the discrete sampling operator, the paper **shows only the slight accuracy fluctuation after discretization (Table 4) and provides no actual latency-acceleration comparison on specific TensorRT or NPU hardware**. Without precise hardware profile data, "completely removing deployment constraints" remains more a qualitative architectural claim; users migrating to a specific embedded platform still need to verify the actual throughput improvement of discrete sampling chip by chip.

### Marginal Contributions Not Decoupled in the Joint Ablation of Multiple Engineering Tricks

In RT-DETRv2's performance report, the combined gains in Table 2 result from dynamic data augmentation, tiered learning rates, sampling-point reduction, and other changes acting together. The paper gives no rigorous single-variable ablation breakdown of each individual change, making it hard for users to precisely isolate the marginal contribution of any one technique.

## Appendix

### Appendix A: Data Flow and Computation Graph of the Hybrid Encoder

The input is the multi-scale feature maps $\{S_3, S_4, S_5\}$ output by the backbone's last three stages.

1. **AIFI processes the high-level features:**
   The high-level feature $S_5 \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C \times H_5 \times W_5}$ is first flattened spatially and transposed into sequence format:
   $$
   \mathbf{X}_5 = \text{Flatten}(S_5) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times (H_5 W_5) \times C}
   $$
   A standard single-layer multi-head self-attention (MHSA) and feed-forward network (FFN) are applied to $\mathbf{X}_5$:
   $$
   \mathbf{F}_5 = \text{Reshape}\Big(\text{FFN}\big(\text{MHSA}(\mathbf{X}_5, \mathbf{X}_5, \mathbf{X}_5) + \mathbf{X}_5\big)\Big) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times C \times H_5 \times W_5}
   $$

2. **CCFM bidirectional cross-scale fusion:**
   - **Top-down aggregation:**
     The high-level feature $\mathbf{F}_5$ is reduced in dimension by a $1 \times 1$ convolution and upsampled by nearest neighbor, then concatenated along channels with $S_4$ and fed into a RepBlock (a multi-branch re-parameterizable $3 \times 3$ convolutional network) to produce a fused intermediate; that intermediate is upsampled again and concatenated and fused with $S_3$;
   - **Bottom-up aggregation:**
     The fused shallow features pass through strided downsampling convolutions and merge upward level by level with the high-level features via skip connections and element-wise addition;
   - The final output is a multi-scale feature sequence $\{O_3, O_4, O_5\}$ with a unified hidden channel dimension, fed to the decoder and query selection module.

### Appendix B: Mathematical Form of the IoU-Aware Classification Loss

In traditional Focal Loss, the binary cross-entropy supervision target $y \in \{0, 1\}$ is discrete. In IoU-aware Query Selection, the model predicts a class-probability vector $\hat{c} \in (0, 1)^K$ and a box $\hat{b} \in (0, 1)^4$ for each encoder feature.

For a candidate feature assigned as a positive sample, let its ground-truth class be $c^*$ and its ground-truth box $b^*$. The geometric overlap scalar for this sample is defined as:
$$
\text{IoU}^* = \text{IoU}(\hat{b}, b^*)
$$

The continuous classification supervision loss for the positive sample on the ground-truth class channel $c^*$ is constructed as:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{pos}}(\hat{c}_{c^*}, \text{IoU}^*) = -\Big|\text{IoU}^* - \hat{c}_{c^*}\Big|^\gamma \cdot \Big(\text{IoU}^* \log(\hat{c}_{c^*}) + (1 - \text{IoU}^*) \log(1 - \hat{c}_{c^*})\Big)
$$
where:

- When the predicted box fully overlaps the ground truth ($\text{IoU}^* \to 1$), the loss degenerates to the standard hard-positive cross-entropy;
- When the predicted box deviates completely from the target ($\text{IoU}^* \to 0$), the loss pulls the target down to 0 even if the class judgment is foreground, suppressing inflated classification activations;
- The modulation factor $|\text{IoU}^* - \hat{c}_{c^*}|^\gamma$ dynamically lowers the gradient weight of samples that already achieve close classification–localization agreement, focusing the model on features where the two differ sharply.

For background or unmatched negative samples, the supervision target keeps the standard hard-negative penalty:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{neg}}(\hat{c}_k) = -\hat{c}_k^\gamma \log(1 - \hat{c}_k)
$$

| **Baseline model** | Original RT-DETR-R18 (with AIFI + CCFM) | 46.5 | 6x training schedule; 217 FPS at T4 FP16. |
| **Training-recipe tuning** | Scale-adaptive learning rate (`1e-4`) + dynamic augmentation cutoff | $+1.4$ (47.9) | Only training-script hyperparameters and the data pipeline change; the inference forward pass is untouched. |
| **Sampling-point reduction** | Total sampling points from 86,400 to 21,600 | $-0.6$ (47.3) | Sampling memory footprint drops about 75%, but no standalone end-to-end latency reduction is reported. |
| **Discrete operator replacement** | Replace `grid_sample` with `discrete_sample` | $-0.1 \sim -0.4$ | Requires 1x fine-tuning with gradient truncation; removes the non-standard operator dependency at TensorRT export. |
