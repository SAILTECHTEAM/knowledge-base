# Anchor Removal and Optional NMS (YOLOX, v8–v11)

:::tip[Study notes]
Companion notes: [YOLOv8](/files/cv/YOLOv8_Note_v1.pdf) · [YOLOv9](/files/cv/YOLOv9_Note_v1.pdf). Earlier YOLOv6/v7 notes ([v6](/files/cv/YOLOv6_Note_v1.pdf), [v7](/files/cv/YOLOv7_Note_v1.pdf)) give background on decoupled heads and dynamic assignment.
:::

In [Part 3](./v6-v7-hardware-training), single-stage object detectors underwent structural hardware-level refinement: structural reparameterization (RepVGG), planned gradient paths (E-ELAN), and coarse-to-fine label assignment pushed feature extraction throughput and training stability to hardware limits.

Yet once backbone and neck latencies were minimized, single-stage architectures encountered two persistent structural bottlenecks:

1. **Anchor-based geometric and hyperparameter overhead**: since YOLOv2 introduced anchor boxes, every grid cell required 3 predefined anchor boxes generated by dataset-specific clustering. This geometric prior added hyperparameter tuning overhead across datasets and multiplied the output tensor channel dimensions. More critically, static IoU thresholding assigned positive and negative samples without regard to the network's actual prediction quality during training.
2. **One-to-many assignment and NMS latency bottlenecks**: single-stage detectors historically matched one ground-truth object to multiple positive priors during training to provide dense gradient supervision. This design forces the network to produce duplicate, overlapping predictions for the same object at inference time, requiring Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) post-processing. With worst-case complexity of $O(N^2)$, non-contiguous memory access patterns, and high sensitivity to score and IoU thresholds, NMS remained the final barrier to true end-to-end real-time deployment.

Between 2021 and 2024, architectures such as YOLOX, YOLOv8, YOLOv9, and YOLOv10 removed anchor priors, transitioned to continuous coordinate distributions, and eliminated NMS bottlenecks at the label assignment level.

This article traces that progression: YOLOX's decoupled head and dynamic assignment (SimOTA); YOLOv8's Task-Aligned Assigner (TAL) and Distribution Focal Loss (DFL); YOLOv9's Programmable Gradient Information (PGI) addressing deep network information degradation; and YOLOv10's consistent dual assignment scheme for NMS-free inference.

---

## YOLOX: Anchor-Free Prediction and Dynamic Assignment

Introduced by Megvii in 2021, **YOLOX** established the foundation for modern single-stage detectors by integrating three core mechanisms onto a YOLOv3-SPP baseline: **decoupled detection heads**, **anchor-free prediction**, and **dynamic label assignment (SimOTA)**.

### Decoupled Detection Head

In earlier YOLO models, classification confidence and bounding box regression shared a single dense convolutional head (coupled head). However, classification and localization have conflicting feature objectives:

- **Classification** requires **translation invariance**, demanding features that remain insensitive to small spatial translations while capturing high-level semantics.
- **Bounding box regression** requires **translation equivariance** and scale sensitivity, depending on low-level spatial geometry such as edges and corners.

Constraining both tasks to a shared convolutional feature map causes gradient conflict during backpropagation: the inner product of the classification gradient $g_{\text{cls}}$ and regression gradient $g_{\text{reg}}$ often becomes negative ($\langle g_{\text{cls}}, g_{\text{reg}} \rangle < 0$), slowing convergence.

YOLOX resolves this conflict with a decoupled head. Below is the structural comparison between the coupled heads of YOLOv3–v5 and the decoupled head of YOLOX:

![Comparison between YOLOv3–v5 coupled head and YOLOX decoupled head](/img/cv_img/yolo-series/yolox-decoupled-head.png)

> Source: Ge et al., YOLOX: Exceeding YOLO Series in 2021, Fig. 3 Decoupled Head, https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08430

The data flow operates across three stages:

1. **Channel reduction base**: a $1 \times 1$ convolution compresses incoming FPN features to 256 channels, reducing cross-scale redundancy.
2. **Task-specific feature transformation**: the feature map splits into two independent branches, each containing two stacked $3 \times 3$ convolutions:
   - **Classification branch**: extracts translation-invariant semantic representations.
   - **Regression and objectness branch**: extracts edge-sensitive and spatially equivariant representations.
3. **Separate prediction outputs**: the classification branch uses a $1 \times 1$ convolution to output $C$ class logits. The regression branch forks into two parallel $1 \times 1$ convolutions: one predicting 4 bounding box coordinates and the other predicting 1 foreground objectness score.

Decoupling accelerated training convergence from 300 epochs to roughly 200 epochs. While the additional $3 \times 3$ convolutions added approximately 1.1 ms of GPU latency, subsequent designs in YOLOv6 and YOLOv8 streamlined the channel dimensions to recover inference speed. For detection head topologies, see [classification heads](../deconstruction/classification-head).

### Anchor-Free Prediction and Multi-Scale Spatial Routing

While anchor boxes in YOLOv2–v5 improved recall on complex object configurations, they introduced engineering trade-offs: every spatial location evaluated $B=3$ anchor priors, producing tens of thousands of candidate outputs and requiring $k$-means clustering whenever transferring to new datasets.

YOLOX replaced anchor boxes with **anchor-free point prediction**:

- **Simplified prediction carriers**: each grid cell predicts a single bounding box parameterized by center offsets $(t_x, t_y)$ and dimensions $(t_w, t_h)$. This reduces candidate predictions to one-third of anchor-based equivalents and simplifies tensor decoding. For parameterizations, see [prediction carriers](../deconstruction/prediction-carrier).
- **Multi-scale spatial routing**: following FCOS-style scale allocation, small objects are assigned to shallow, high-resolution feature maps (stride 8), while large objects route to deeper, low-resolution maps (stride 32). Center sampling restricts positive candidate locations to a $3 \times 3$ neighborhood around the ground-truth center, filtering out ambiguous edge pixels.

### Dynamic Label Assignment: From OTA to SimOTA

Traditional detectors rely on static geometric criteria (such as a fixed IoU threshold of 0.5) to separate positive and negative samples. Static assignment evaluates overlap against predefined anchors without considering the network's current prediction state: an anchor with modest geometric overlap that produces high prediction accuracy may be discarded as negative, while a poorly localized anchor with high initial IoU receives positive gradients.

Megvii's Optimal Transport Assignment (OTA) framed label assignment as an **optimal transport problem**: $m$ ground-truth objects act as suppliers offering positive label units, $n$ feature points act as demanders requesting supervision, and the transport cost represents the combined classification and regression loss of the current prediction.

#### The SimOTA Algorithm

Standard OTA requires the iterative Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, increasing training runtime by over 25%. YOLOX introduced **SimOTA**, a dynamic Top-$k$ approximation executed in four steps:

1. **Matching cost matrix**:
   For prediction point $i$ and ground-truth object $j$, the matching cost is:

   $$c_{ij} = L_{\text{cls}}(p_i, g_j^{\text{cls}}) + \lambda L_{\text{reg}}(b_i, g_j^{\text{box}})$$

   where $L_{\text{cls}}$ is binary cross-entropy (BCE), $L_{\text{reg}}$ is negative log-IoU ($-\ln(\text{IoU})$), and $\lambda = 3.0$ balances loss scales. Higher prediction accuracy yields a lower cost $c_{ij}$.
2. **Center prior filtering**:
   Positive candidate selections $\Omega_j$ are restricted to feature points falling within a spatial radius of $1.5 \times \text{stride}$ from the ground-truth center.
3. **Dynamic $k$ estimation**:
   Rather than fixing the number of positive samples per ground truth, SimOTA estimates capacity dynamically by summing the IoU values of the top 10 highest-overlap candidates:

   $$k_j = \text{clamp}\left( \left\lfloor \sum_{m \in \text{Top10\_IoU}} \text{IoU}(b_m, g_j) \right\rfloor, \min=1 \right)$$

   For distinct, easily detected objects, top IoU values are high and sum to larger positive allocations ($k_j \approx 7$–$8$). For occluded or distorted objects, low IoU values yield smaller allocations ($k_j \approx 1$–$2$), preventing low-quality predictions from corrupting gradients.
4. **Global cost ranking and conflict resolution**:
   Each ground truth selects the $k_j$ candidates with the lowest cost $c_{ij}$. If a candidate point is claimed by multiple ground truths, it is assigned exclusively to the ground truth with the minimum absolute cost $c_{ij}$.

For the full assignment mechanics, see [label assignment](../deconstruction/label-assignment).

In design terms, YOLOX's central goal was shedding the historical baggage of anchor priors and coupled heads. The authors accepted the extra inference latency (roughly 1.1 ms) from dense $3 \times 3$ convolutions in the standard decoupled head in exchange for faster convergence under anchor-free prediction and stronger cross-dataset generalization — establishing the baseline for subsequent lightweight head refinements.

## YOLOv8: Decoupled Representations, Continuous Distributions, and Task Alignment

Released by Ultralytics in 2023, **YOLOv8** standardized the modern single-stage detection pipeline by eliminating legacy Darknet conventions and introducing task-aligned assignment and continuous bounding box distributions.

### Task-Aligned Assigner (TAL) and Elimination of the Objectness Branch

In YOLOX, classification and localization branches compute losses independently despite shared cost modeling in SimOTA. When the two heads converge at different rates, **prediction misalignment** occurs:

- A predicted box may achieve high classification confidence despite poor spatial localization.
- A well-localized box may receive a lower classification score.

Because NMS sorts candidate boxes strictly by classification score, poorly localized boxes with high confidence scores are preserved, suppressing accurately localized candidates with lower scores. This degrades high-threshold metrics ($\text{AP}_{75}$, $\text{AP}_{90}$).

#### Alignment Metric

YOLOv8 integrates the **Task-Aligned Assigner (TAL)**, computing an explicit alignment metric $t$:

$$t = s^\alpha \cdot u^\beta$$

where $s \in [0, 1]$ is the predicted class score, $u \in [0, 1]$ is the IoU between predicted and ground-truth boxes, and hyperparameters are set to $\alpha = 1.0, \beta = 6.0$.

The high exponent $\beta = 6.0$ penalizes poor localization: even if $s = 1.0$, a drop in IoU to $u = 0.7$ reduces the alignment metric to $t = 1.0 \times 0.7^6 \approx 0.117$. Only predictions that excel in both classification and localization receive high alignment scores. The cost of this strict setting: for occluded or blurred hard samples, even perfectly accurate classification receives a sharply suppressed supervision weight if box localization deviates slightly, risking reduced recall on difficult objects. The authors accepted this constraint to eliminate, at the source, the suppression of well-localized boxes by high-scoring but poorly localized predictions in traditional NMS.

#### Dynamic Soft-Label Supervision

TAL uses $t$ to select the top candidates (defaulting to 13) and converts $t$ into continuous soft supervision targets:

1. **Soft classification targets**: classification BCE loss uses normalized $t$ as a continuous soft target rather than hard binary labels $\{0, 1\}$.
2. **Alignment-weighted regression**: the CIoU bounding box loss is dynamically weighted by $t$ ($L_{\text{box}} = t \cdot L_{\text{CIoU}}$), concentrating localization gradients on well-aligned candidates.

**Why YOLOv8 eliminates the objectness branch**: in earlier architectures, final detection scores were the product of the objectness score and class probability. Under TAL, the soft target $t$ directly reflects spatial overlap and class confidence simultaneously. Unaligned background locations naturally converge to $t \to 0$. A standalone objectness branch is therefore redundant and removed, reducing head parameter count.

### Continuous Bounding Box Distributions: Distribution Focal Loss (DFL)

Standard regression models bounding box coordinates as Dirac delta distributions, treating ground-truth boundaries as single deterministic values.

In real-world images, boundaries often contain spatial ambiguity caused by motion blur, low lighting, occlusion, and annotation noise. Fitting a single coordinate cannot capture this uncertainty.

![Continuous bounding box probability distribution modeling: wide flat distribution for ambiguous and occluded edges, sharp unimodal peak for clear edges](/img/cv_img/yolo-series/gfl-dfl-distribution.png)

> Source: Li et al., Generalized Focal Loss: Learning Qualified and Distributed Bounding Boxes for Dense Object Detection, Fig. 3, https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04388

#### Integral Expectation Regression and DFL

YOLOv8 integrates **Distribution Focal Loss (DFL)**, modeling coordinate offset $y$ relative to the anchor point as a general probability distribution over a discrete range $[0, \text{reg\_max}]$:

1. **Distribution output and expectation decoding**:
   Instead of predicting 4 scalar coordinates, the regression branch predicts a logit vector of length $\text{reg\_max} = 16$ per coordinate. After softmax normalization into probabilities $P = [P_0, P_1, \dots, P_{15}]$, the continuous coordinate is recovered via mathematical expectation:

   $$\hat{y} = \sum_{i=0}^{\text{reg\_max}-1} P_i \cdot i$$

   This operation is implemented as a fixed $1 \times 1$ convolution with non-trainable weights $[0, 1, 2, \dots, 15]$.
2. **DFL loss formulation**:
   For continuous float label $y$ bounded by adjacent integers $y_i \le y < y_{i+1}$ (where $y_{i+1} = y_i + 1$), DFL forces probability mass to concentrate on the nearest integer bins:

   $$L_{\text{DFL}} = -\left( (y_{i+1} - y) \ln(P_i) + (y - y_i) \ln(P_{i+1}) \right)$$

   Clear object boundaries produce sharp, unimodal distributions resembling Dirac deltas; ambiguous or occluded edges yield broader distributions reflecting localization uncertainty. The cost of this design was expanding bounding-box outputs to $4 \times 16 = 64$ channels per grid cell and introducing Softmax plus integral-expectation operators at inference. The authors abandoned the minimalist alternative of regressing 4 scalars directly in exchange for localization accuracy on ambiguous and occluded boundaries.

For loss derivations and boundary parameterizations, see [box regression](../deconstruction/bbox-regression-loss).

### Backbone Evolution: Multi-Level Feature Reuse in C2f

YOLOv8 replaced the C3 module of YOLOv5 with the **C2f (CSP Bottleneck with 2 Convolutions)** module across the backbone and neck.

For input tensor $X \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C_{\text{in}}}$:

1. A $1 \times 1$ convolution expands channels to $2C$, split evenly into $x_1, x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$.
2. Branch $x_1$ acts as a direct shortcut, while $x_2$ passes through a sequence of $n$ bottleneck residual units.
3. Rather than concatenating only the final layer output (as in C3), **C2f concatenates the output of every intermediate bottleneck unit**:

   $$Y = \text{Conv}_{1 \times 1}\left( [x_1, x_2, \text{Bottleneck}_1(x_2), \text{Bottleneck}_2(\dots), \dots] \right)$$

Drawing from DenseNet and ELAN gradient concepts, C2f shortens backpropagation paths between shallow and deep features, improving gradient propagation and multi-scale feature reuse. For CSP variants, see [neck architectures](../deconstruction/neck-architecture).

YOLOv8's principal architectural trade-offs were concentrated in the detection head: DFL distribution modeling traded $4 \times 16 = 64$-channel expansion and inference-side Softmax integration operators for fine-grained modeling of occluded and ambiguous edges, while TAL delegated sample matching to joint classification–localization scoring, trading stricter geometric penalties for cleaner gradients on high-quality samples. The C3-to-C2f backbone upgrade, by contrast, was a pure structural optimization improving feature reuse at comparable compute.

## YOLOv9: Information Bottlenecks and Programmable Gradient Information (PGI)

As single-stage detection networks grew deeper, feature representations suffered from information degradation during forward propagation. Developed in 2024, **YOLOv9** analyzed this breakdown using **information theory and the data processing inequality**, introducing **Programmable Gradient Information (PGI)**.

### Information Degradation and the Data Processing Inequality

In deep feedforward networks, input image $X$ undergoes sequential strided convolutions, downsampling operations, and non-linear activations, forming a Markov chain: $X \to f_1(X) \to f_2(f_1(X)) \dots \to Y$. By the **data processing inequality**:

$$I(X; X) \ge I(X; f_1(X)) \ge I(X; f_2(f_1(X))) \ge \dots \ge I(X; Y)$$

The mutual information $I(X; \cdot)$ between intermediate feature representations and the original input degrades monotonically with depth. Every strided downsampling and non-linear activation discards fine-grained spatial and high-frequency details.

When networks become deep or operate under constrained channel widths, deep detection heads receive degraded features lacking critical geometric and semantic cues. Gradients $\nabla_\theta L$ computed from these degraded representations diverge from optimal update directions.

While **reversible neural networks** preserve mutual information through bijective mappings ($X = \psi^{-1}(\zeta(X))$), forcing every backbone layer to remain strictly reversible adds 20%–50% inference compute, reducing execution speed.

### Programmable Gradient Information (PGI) Architecture

![PGI programmable gradient information architecture design](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13616v2/figs/pgi.png)

> Source: Wang et al., YOLOv9: Learning What You Want to Learn Using Programmable Gradient Information, Fig. 3 PGI, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13616

YOLOv9 resolves this by **decoupling inference feature compression from training gradient backpropagation**. PGI comprises three parallel components:

1. **Main branch**: optimized exclusively for inference. It uses standard downsampling layers to minimize compute, operating without auxiliary branches at runtime.
2. **Auxiliary reversible branch**: active only during training. Constructed from reversible blocks processing shallow, high-resolution features, it retains full mutual information. Gradients $\frac{\partial L_{\text{aux}}}{\partial \theta}$ computed from this branch provide accurate supervision directly to backbone layers, correcting gradient drift in the main branch.
3. **Multi-level auxiliary information**: standard deep supervision routes ground-truth signals only to corresponding scale layers, causing shallow layers to treat larger objects as background. Multi-level auxiliary networks aggregate multi-scale target features before backpropagating to shallow layers, ensuring early layers retain holistic object representations.

At inference time, the auxiliary reversible branch and multi-level integration paths are discarded. Combined with the **Generalized Efficient Layer Aggregation Network (GELAN)**, YOLOv9 matches or exceeds prior accuracy while reducing parameter counts.

Architecturally, YOLOv9 extended the front-load-training-cost design philosophy. The authors accepted the extra memory footprint and backpropagation compute of maintaining auxiliary reversible branches and multi-level auxiliary connections during training in exchange for a compact inference backbone with zero added overhead — avoiding the heavy forward latency of a fully reversible backbone.

## YOLOv10: End-to-End NMS-Free Detection via Consistent Dual Assignments

While YOLOX through YOLOv9 established anchor-free designs and dynamic matching, they remained constrained by post-processing NMS latency. Introduced by Tsinghua University researchers in 2024, **YOLOv10** eliminated NMS dependencies using **consistent dual assignments**.

### The One-to-Many vs. One-to-One Dilemma

The root cause of redundant predictions lies in the training assignment strategy:

- **One-to-many assignment (o2m)**: YOLOv1–v9 assigned multiple prediction points to each ground-truth object during training. This provides rich gradient supervision, speeding up convergence and boosting recall. However, it forces the network to output multiple overlapping candidate boxes per object at inference, requiring NMS.
- **One-to-one assignment (o2o)**: end-to-end detectors like DETR use bipartite matching to assign exactly one prediction per ground truth. Inference is naturally NMS-free, but sparse supervision leads to slow optimization convergence and degraded small-object accuracy on convolutional architectures.

### Consistent Dual Assignments

YOLOv10 trains two parallel detection heads sharing a common backbone and neck:

- **One-to-many auxiliary head**: trained with TAL one-to-many dynamic assignment, providing rich positive supervision to the shared backbone.
- **One-to-one main head**: trained with one-to-one assignment (matching the top-1 aligned prediction per ground truth), producing non-redundant predictions.

![YOLOv10 consistent dual assignments and metric consistency](https://ar5iv.org/html/2405.14458/assets/nms.png)

> Source: Wang et al., YOLOv10: Real-Time End-to-End Object Detection, Fig. 2 Consistent dual assignments, https://ar5iv.org/html/2405.14458

#### Harmonizing Supervision via Metric Consistency

Training two heads naively causes **supervision conflict**: a prediction point treated as positive by the one-to-many head may be treated as negative background by the one-to-one head, creating opposing gradient signals on the shared backbone.

Let the matching metric for prediction $i$ be $m$. The supervision disparity between heads is:

$$\mathcal{A} = t_{\text{o2o}, i} - \mathbb{I}(i \in \Omega_{\text{o2m}}) t_{\text{o2m}, i} + \sum_{k \neq i, k \in \Omega_{\text{o2m}}} t_{\text{o2m}, k}$$

where $\Omega_{\text{o2m}}$ is the set of positive samples selected by the one-to-many assigner. To eliminate gradient conflict, **the optimal positive sample selected by the one-to-one head must match the top-ranked candidate of the one-to-many head**.

YOLOv10 enforces this through a **consistent matching metric**:

$$m(\alpha, \beta) = s \cdot p^\alpha \cdot \text{IoU}(\hat{b}, b)^\beta$$

where $s$ is a spatial center prior and $p$ is the class probability. By scaling the assigner hyperparameters proportionally ($\alpha_{\text{o2o}} = r \alpha_{\text{o2m}}$ and $\beta_{\text{o2o}} = r \beta_{\text{o2m}}$), the two metrics satisfy:

$$m_{\text{o2o}} = \left( m_{\text{o2m}} \right)^r$$

Because power transformations over positive reals are strictly monotonic, they preserve candidate ranking order:

$$\arg\max_i m_{\text{o2o}} \equiv \arg\max_i m_{\text{o2m}}$$

This ensures that the positive sample chosen by the one-to-one head is identically the highest-ranked prediction in the one-to-many head. The two heads optimize without gradient conflict.

During deployment, **the one-to-many auxiliary head is discarded, and only the one-to-one main head is retained**, achieving real-time **NMS-free inference**.

In architectural terms, YOLOv10's core trade-off spent dual-head training compute and memory for deterministic zero-NMS latency at inference. Because training directly with one-to-one matching would starve convolutional networks of positive-sample gradients and severely degrade small-object performance, the authors absorbed that optimization difficulty entirely into training through a provably consistent dual-assignment mechanism.

### Efficiency Design via Numerical Rank Analysis

YOLOv10 also analyzes computational redundancy by computing the **numerical rank** of convolutional feature maps using singular value decomposition (SVD):

- Deep stages and large model variants exhibit lower numerical rank, indicating high channel correlation and feature redundancy.
- YOLOv10 replaces standard convolutions in low-rank stages with **Compact Inverted Blocks (CIB)** using depthwise separable convolutions.
- Introducing channel-decoupled downsampling and lightweight classification heads reduces end-to-end latency by over 46% while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## YOLOv11: Operator Refinement and Multi-Task Integration

Released by Ultralytics in late 2024, **YOLOv11** refined micro-architectural components and unified multi-task support:

1. **C3k2 feature extraction module**: a modular update to C2f allowing selectable kernel sizes ($k=3$ or smaller kernels) within residual bottlenecks, improving memory access locality on edge devices.
2. **Polarized self-attention (C2PSA)**: positioned after the top-level SPPF module in the neck, C2PSA applies spatial and channel polarization to capture global contextual dependencies with low computational overhead.
3. **Depthwise separable classification heads**: standard $3 \times 3$ convolutions in decoupled classification heads are replaced with depthwise separable convolutions, reducing parameters on datasets with many categories.
4. **Unified multi-task pipeline**: consolidates object detection, instance segmentation, pose estimation, oriented bounding box detection (OBB), and tracking into a single codebase.

## Summary: The Modern Single-Stage Detection Paradigm

From YOLOX in 2021 to YOLOv10/v11 in 2024, single-stage object detection transitioned into a standardized design pattern:

| Dimension | Legacy Pattern (v2–v5) | Modern Pattern (YOLOX–v11) | Core Mechanism and Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prediction carrier** | Clustered anchor priors | **Anchor-free point prediction** | Removes manual geometric tuning; cuts candidate count by two-thirds, speeding up decode. |
| **Head architecture** | Coupled head | **Decoupled head** | Resolves gradient conflict between classification and localization. |
| **Label assignment** | Static IoU thresholds | **Dynamic alignment (SimOTA / TAL)** | Assigns samples based on prediction quality; aligns classification with localization. |
| **Box representation** | Deterministic Dirac delta coordinates | **Continuous distribution (DFL)** | Models boundary ambiguity under occlusion and blur to improve localization quality. |
| **Deep optimization** | Feature truncation / standard deep supervision | **Programmable gradients (PGI) / dual guidance** | Compensates for information loss under the data processing inequality via lossless gradient paths. |
| **Inference deployment** | Greedy NMS post-processing | **Consistent dual assignment (NMS-free)** | Eliminates redundant predictions at the assignment level, removing edge latency bottlenecks. |

With anchor-free representations, dynamic assignment, and NMS-free dual heads in place, the convolutional detection framework achieved structural maturity. However, expanding deployments into high-resolution imagery, vision-language open-vocabulary detection, and extreme edge microcontrollers revealed new architectural trade-offs:

1. **Global context modeling vs. real-time latency**: how to integrate Vision Transformer (ViT) self-attention into real-time backbones without exceeding mobile latency budgets.
2. **Operator minimalism on edge microcontrollers**: how to simplify 64-dimensional DFL outputs and multi-branch heads for low-power microcontrollers (MCUs) that lack complex kernel support.
